### THE INQUISITOR'S ASSESSMENT (Canon & Setting Inspector)

**Strengths:**

Sub-Station Seven's placement aligns with actual NJ Transit rail maps. The telluric magic system integrates plausibly into this industrial landscape; cold-iron wards at transit hubs read as both historically resonant and functionally logical within the narrative framework.

The absence of anachronisms is notable—no smartphones, no social media mentions, no modern surveillance tech. This era-grounding succeeds where many dark fantasy novels fail.

**Critical Flaws:**
- **Lexical inconsistency:** "Rune-carved shotgun" appears multiple times despite cold-iron being explicitly stated as resistant to magical modification (p. 45 vs. p. 137). Either cold-iron should accept runes or this contradiction must be resolved.
- **Telluric logic gaps:** Chapter 8 introduces "Apep nails" without establishing their function or relation to the existing magic system until page 231—a significant information delay that breaks immersion.
- **Weather implausibility:** November rain maintaining constant downpour for 48+ hours across Chapters 1-6 contradicts New Jersey meteorological patterns. Even accounting for supernatural influence, this needs acknowledgment in-world or moderation.

**Recommendations:**
Establish a magic-system appendix clarifying cold-iron properties versus rune-work compatibility. Address the temporal implausibility of sustained precipitation.
